# Teaching

<!-- Fall 2019: Prof. Kara is on sabbatical, hence not teaching. -->

I believe engineering students learn computational methods best when they work through both the mathematics and the implementation. In my courses, students derive methods, write code, examine where the methods fail, and use them on engineering problems. I place particular emphasis on helping students become comfortable with problems that initially appear difficult or unfamiliar.

Over the years, I have developed and taught courses in artificial intelligence, machine learning, numerical methods, engineering design, and computational engineering. The examples and assignments in these courses often grow out of problems I have encountered in my own research.

## Selected curricular contributions

### Artificial intelligence and machine learning for engineers

I developed **24-787 Machine Learning and Artificial Intelligence for Engineers** and first taught it in 2008. My goal was to give engineering students a rigorous introduction to machine learning without treating the methods as black boxes. Students study the mathematical basis of each method and then implement it themselves. I have been especially pleased to see students build on their course projects in publications and graduate theses.

I later developed **24-887**, the online version of the course, for CMU's graduate certificate in AI Engineering Fundamentals. For this course, I created a new set of lecture materials, videos, computational exercises, and learn-by-doing activities designed specifically for online students.

I also developed a project course in which student teams choose a real engineering problem, define its requirements and constraints, and build an AI or machine-learning system to address it. The projects have covered a wide range of engineering applications, and several have developed into research publications.

### Numerical and computational engineering

I began teaching **24-311 Numerical Methods** in Spring 2026 and undertook a substantial redesign of the course. I moved the programming work from MATLAB to Python, expanded the optimization material, and added introductory machine-learning topics. I also developed a complete set of lecture slides and Python demonstrations that I use live in class.

I developed **24-282 Linear Algebra and Vector Calculus** after recognizing that our undergraduate students needed a stronger foundation in these subjects before taking numerical methods and other computational courses. The course later became a requirement in the Mechanical Engineering curriculum and was adopted more broadly by the College of Engineering.

### Engineering design and hands-on learning

When teaching **24-441 Engineering Design: Conceptualization and Realization**, I asked teams to begin physical prototyping early rather than waiting until the end of the semester. I organized the course around short project milestones, prototype reviews, peer feedback, and evaluations by an external “red team.” I found that this structure helped students identify problems sooner and maintain steady progress throughout the semester.

I first taught **24-352 Dynamic Systems and Control** during the pandemic. To preserve a hands-on component, we sent each student an Arduino, a motor, and supporting circuit components. Students used the kits for system identification, step-response measurements, and PID control. The module worked well enough that it continued to be used after the course returned to in-person instruction.

## Teaching recognition

I received the **Philip and Marsha Dowd Fellowship** for my work on CMU's online certificate in AI Engineering Fundamentals. I have also received multiple **Professor of the Year** awards from the graduating class of the Department of Mechanical Engineering.
